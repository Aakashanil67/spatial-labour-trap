## Resource balance reconciles exactly, not approximately

Every agent, every step, across all three configs and three seeds: opening capital + credited
household inflow − paid search cost = closing capital. **2,700,000 agent-steps checked, zero
violations, maximum discrepancy 0.0.** Confirmed by reasoning through the code first (the only
two per-step mutation sites are `agents.py`'s `step_searching` cost deduction and
`step_discouraged`'s inflow credit; employment touches capital nowhere), then verifying the
identity holds with no exceptions rather than assuming it.

## The published-window correction is not cosmetic

The original A7 diagnostic averaged its first window over steps 1-150, which includes the
model's own cold-start transient (documented as settling by roughly step 50). Recomputed
correctly over 51-150 (excluding burn-in, matching what the calibration itself measures over):

| config | old window (1-150) | corrected window (51-150) | difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| inflow 0.004 | 0.1151 | 0.1700 | +0.0548 |
| inflow 0.008 (published) | 0.0883 | 0.1303 | +0.0420 |
| inflow 0.016 | 0.0632 | 0.0931 | +0.0300 |

The transient-contaminated window understates the broad-inactive share by 0.030 to 0.055 --
30 to 40 per cent relative to the corrected figure. Any comparison against a post-burn-in
calibration moment using the old window would have been comparing two different populations
without saying so. This is exactly the risk the plan flagged; it is real, not hypothetical.

## Employment is vacancy-constrained, not searcher-constrained, at this fitted point

An unplanned finding, surfaced by comparing the three household-inflow arms directly rather than
only their discouraged-share series. `employment_share` (equivalently, the raw `l` series) is
**bit-for-bit identical across all three household_inflow settings**, at every step, for a given
seed -- not merely similar. `hard_discouraged_share` and `mean_search_capital` do vary
meaningfully across the same arms, confirming the instrumentation is working and the arms are
genuinely different runs.

The mechanism: mean vacancies (`v`) over the published window are essentially identical across
arms (6.60 in each), while mean searchers (`u`) range from 233.7 (low inflow) to 269.4 (high
inflow) -- a searching pool 35 to 40 times the vacancy count in every arm. Total matches over the
published window are identical too (660 in every arm). With searchers this far in excess of
vacancies in all three cases, the number of hires is set entirely by the vacancy-posting side
(the shared productivity shock and firm behaviour, unaffected by `household_inflow`), and never
by whether the searching pool is 234 or 269 people.

**What this means for the model as a whole, not just this sensitivity check.** Employment
dynamics at this fitted point cannot respond to anything that changes only the size of the
searching pool -- only to something that changes vacancy supply. This is direct evidence for
Astra's numerical-safeguards concern (five firms, integer vacancy rounding, a hiring-rate floor
can materially dominate outcomes): here, they do not just plausibly dominate, they are the
*entire* determinant of the employment level, to bit-for-bit precision, at least across this
range of `household_inflow`. Two consequences worth flagging now rather than after the policy
comparison is built:

- A transport subsidy that operates by making more people able to afford search trips (the
  channel this thesis's whole mechanism runs on) may have **no effect on the employment level**
  if it only ever changes the size of an already-vacancy-saturated searching pool. It could still
  move discouragement, inactivity and search intensity -- which are real outcomes -- but the
  headline "additional employment per rand" comparison the research question is built around
  could be structurally muted by this constraint, independent of how well-designed the subsidy
  is.
- This is a testable, falsifiable prediction, not a conjecture: if the transport-subsidy policy
  experiment (P5) also shows employment invariant to the subsidy rate while vacancies stay near
  their current level, that would confirm the same vacancy bottleneck is operating there. If firm
  posting responds enough to the resulting larger searching pool (via `expected_value_per_hire`)
  to lift vacancies, the bottleneck could loosen. Worth checking directly once the policy engine
  exists, rather than discovering it only when the headline comparison comes back flat.

## Composition finding from A7 confirmed, not superseded

The never-employed share of the non-employed population still falls from close to 1 toward 0
over the horizon in every arm (visible in `horizon_diagnostic.png`), consistent with A7's
original finding. Nothing here changes the earlier conclusion that this is real evidence of a
compositional mechanism, or the earlier correction that it does not by itself establish or rule
out a stationary distribution -- P1.2 extends the horizon further and varies initial conditions
to probe that question, which this task does not attempt to answer.

## What this does not do

This is P1.1's reproduction, not P1.2's extended assessment. It does not run past 600 steps,
does not vary initial capital, and does not compare initialisation schemes. It also does not
settle G1 -- the temporal interpretation is still open, per `DECISIONS_PENDING.md` question 1.
